# Categorization lenses

These help a participant turn a vague Working/Friction/Spark note into something specific enough to act on. They are naming tools, not solutioning tools — a lens tells you *what kind* of problem you're looking at, never what to do about it. Offer these to participants as an optional aid, not a requirement; a plain, specific note beats a jargon-tagged vague one every time.

## Severity tags (P0-P3)

Tag the weight of a Friction note:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P0 | Blocking — stops the task entirely |
| P1 | Major — causes real difficulty or confusion |
| P2 | Minor — an annoyance with a workaround |
| P3 | Polish — nice to address, no real user impact |

Quick test: would a user contact support about this? If yes, it's at least P1.

## Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics

Useful for naming *why* something feels off, especially for Friction notes:

1. Visibility of system status
2. Match between system and the real world
3. User control and freedom
4. Consistency and standards
5. Error prevention
6. Recognition rather than recall
7. Flexibility and efficiency of use
8. Aesthetic and minimalist design
9. Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors
10. Help and documentation

A note like "nav feels confusing" becomes "Recognition Rather Than Recall — icon-only nav with no labels."

## Craft rubric dimensions

Useful for Working and Friction notes about visual and interaction craft:

Hierarchy & layout · typography · spacing & rhythm · color & contrast · motion · component states · accessibility · responsiveness · content & copy · consistency & brand

## Persona lenses

Useful for Spark notes — walking the design through a specific user's eyes often surfaces what a general look misses. Pick 2-3 relevant to the interface:

- **Power user** — expert, wants speed and shortcuts, abandons anything that feels slow or hand-holding
- **First-timer** — never seen this pattern before, needs things spelled out, abandons rather than guesses
- **Accessibility-dependent user** — screen reader or keyboard-only, needs labels, contrast, and focus states to work
- **Stress tester** — pushes edge cases: empty states, huge inputs, refresh mid-flow
- **Mobile user** — one thumb, on the go, easily interrupted, needs state preserved and controls in reach

A Spark note might read: "Spark — a first-timer walking this flow cold might not know the icon-only step means 'in progress.' Worth testing with someone new to the pattern."
